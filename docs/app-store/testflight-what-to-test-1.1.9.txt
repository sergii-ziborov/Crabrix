Crabrix 1.1.9: Rust for C and Node.js developers, learning with your AI agent, the code screen on iPhone and real questions on flashcards. Everything from 1.1.8 is included.

New courses for developers who already know another language: in Learn, download "Rust for C Developers" or "Rust for Node.js Developers". Each lesson shows the C or JavaScript/TypeScript code next to the Rust version (a C / Rust switch on iPhone, side by side on iPad), lists what changes, and most lessons ask you to port the function to Rust. Finishing a lesson marks the Academy lessons it covers as "Known from C" or "Known from Node.js": they open the path and count toward course progress, but give no XP or achievements, and their questions still come back in Flashcards. Please check that the right lessons are marked and that resetting the bridge course takes the marks back. Both courses start with "Rust at a glance": how Rust differs in general (compiled ahead of time, no garbage collector, what safe Rust rules out, reading rustc errors).

Learn with AI (optional): at the bottom of Learn, open "Learn with your AI agent". Pick Claude, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or another client and copy its setup step or a prompt. Every lesson has a sparkles button, "Learn this lesson with AI", with a prompt that holds only that lesson's public details. Under Continue learning, "Include my completed lessons" is off by default; switch it on to share your learning state with your agent. Please check that nothing in your progress, answers or projects changes, and that the app works as before without AI and offline.

Rust Basics now starts with "What Rust Is". After Update all, an existing learner's Basics path points to this short lesson first; all earlier progress is kept.

Code screen on iPhone in portrait: while a project is open, the Projects, Learn and Settings tabs are hidden and Code, Problems, Output and Terminal take their place at the bottom of the screen. Close the editor with × at the top to return to Projects. In landscape and on iPad and Duo the layout did not change.

Badges: Problems shows how many compiler problems there are. Output shows how many new results arrived while you were on another tab, and Terminal how many new Git messages. Opening the tab clears its badge.

Flashcards: in Learn, update your courses (Update all), finish a lesson and open Flashcards. The generic "What is the core idea of …?" card is gone. Each lesson now asks its own questions: two per Rust lesson and one per Algorithms step, plus the lesson's Quick Check. Please report any question that is unclear or any answer you think is wrong.
